## This week's items

**1. Forty Percent of Employees Say Their CEO Isn't Transparent Enough on AI**
Outlet: Boston Consulting Group (press release, CEO Insomnia Index) | Published: October 14, 2026
Link: https://www.bcg.com/press/14october2026-employees-ceo-transparent-ai
Synopsis: BCG surveyed 645 CEOs and 500 non-executive employees. About 40 percent of employees say their CEO has not been open enough about how AI will affect jobs and careers, while few CEOs worry their AI approach could damage trust. Employees whose trust in their CEO's AI leadership grew were far more likely to say they are going beyond their duties.
Why it matters: This is the closest data match to your lead angle this week: the gap is about being told, and trust in how AI is handled shows up in discretionary effort.
Suggested action: Cite. Note that this is BCG's own release about its own survey, not independent reporting; lead with the numbers and name the source.

**2. Littler Survey: AI and Leadership Communication Shape Labor Relations in 2026**
Outlet: IndustryWeek (Anna Smith) | Published: October 14, 2026
Link: https://www.industryweek.com/talent/labor-employment-policy/news/55411724/littler-survey-ai-and-leadership-communication-shape-labor-relations-in-2026
Synopsis: Littler's employer survey finds few organizations communicate proactively and consistently with employees about how AI will affect their roles, and very few describe themselves as very prepared for AI-related employee relations issues. A majority of respondents expect unions to use fears of AI job displacement to build organizing interest.
Why it matters: It quantifies the "last to know" problem from the employer side and adds a business-risk reason (organizing, retention) for fixing it.
Suggested action: Cite or share with a one-line take. The earlier Canadian Lawyer coverage of the same survey (October 2) was in last week's statistics; this is a fuller write-up.

**3. HR leaders may be able to use AI to improve employee wellness**
Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen) | Published: October 14, 2026
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/hr-leaders-may-be-able-to-use-ai-to-improve-employee-wellness/832960/
Synopsis: Drawing on a Gartner survey of nearly 12,000 employees, the article says AI could help HR spot early signs of disengagement and burnout, and could also contribute to those problems. Almost half of workers who were asked to use AI at work say they have resisted adopting it.
Why it matters: It gives you a number on resistance, which you can reframe: resistance is often a signal about information and trust, not about the technology.
Suggested action: Comment or cite. Be careful to describe the finding as resistance among workers asked to use AI, as the article does.

**4. Kroger To Cut 37% Of Corporate Jobs, Another AI Win**
Outlet: 24/7 Wall St. (Douglas A. McIntyre) | Published: October 13, 2026
Link: https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/13/kroger-to-cut-37-of-corporate-jobs-another-ai-win/
Synopsis: The article reports Kroger plans to cut roughly 3,000 corporate roles, concentrated in middle management, and says employees heard through an internal message from the CEO. The CEO is reported to have said AI is making management work more efficient, which the article treats as the efficiency argument behind the cuts.
Why it matters: When an AI efficiency line and a large layoff arrive in the same message, employees will connect the two whether or not the company intends it.
Suggested action: Comment on LinkedIn with a communications read on how the CEO message was framed. Caution: I read only this secondary article, which cites a Bloomberg report; I have not seen the CEO's note or the Bloomberg piece, and the headline's "AI win" framing is the author's.

**5. Tech layoffs 2026 update: Job losses pile up at Netflix, HubSpot, Flock Safety, and others in October**
Outlet: Fast Company (Katie Mather) | Published: October 16, 2026
Link: https://fastcompany.com/91625107/tech-layoffs-2026-update-mass-job-cuts-october-netflix-hubspot-flock-more
Synopsis: A roundup of October cuts at Netflix, HubSpot, Flock Safety, Airtable and Amazon, with each company giving a different explanation: restructuring, post-acquisition changes, a voluntary buyout, over-hiring. HubSpot's CEO is reported to have told staff the cuts were "not driven by AI-related efficiencies."
Why it matters: Side by side, the explanations show how differently companies frame the same event, and how often AI is either claimed or explicitly denied.
Suggested action: Share or skip. Useful as a reference list if you write about how layoff messages are framed; HubSpot was covered in last week's synopsis.

**6. California Enacts Two New AI in Employment Bills: What's Coming in 2027**
Outlet: Foley & Lardner (law firm publication, Sara Alexis Levine Abarbanel) | Published: October 12, 2026
Link: https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/10/california-enacts-two-new-ai-in-employment-bills-whats-coming-in-2027/
Synopsis: Two California bills signed by the governor take effect January 1, 2027. One limits relying solely on automated decision systems for discipline or termination and requires a plain-language notice to the employee afterward; the other adds detail about automation-driven layoffs to the state's mass-layoff notice, including which job functions are being automated.
Why it matters: The law is beginning to require the kind of disclosure you advocate: telling affected people plainly that AI was involved and what it did.
Suggested action: Cite for the regulatory angle. Note this is a law-firm summary, not a reading of the statutes. The earlier October 12 synopsis cited a Morgan Lewis piece on California's AI laws; this is a different publication on what appear to be related laws, and I have not compared the two.

## Pattern of the week
The week's strongest theme is a perception gap between leaders and employees. BCG finds employees far less convinced than CEOs that trust is in place, and Littler finds most employers are not communicating proactively about AI's effect on roles. Layoff coverage shows the second pattern worth watching: companies are split on whether to tie cuts to AI. Kroger's message is reported to link management cuts to AI efficiency, while HubSpot's CEO is reported to have separated the two. Nothing this week cuts directly against your angle, but the Gartner item adds a nuance: some workers resist AI adoption, so "people do not resist AI" needs to be phrased carefully, for example as resistance being driven by what is unsaid more than by the tool itself. That reframing is my suggestion, not something the sources claim.
