## This week's items

**1. HubSpot cuts about 660 jobs while revenue and profit are growing**
Outlet: TheStreet (Aparajita Chatterjee) | Published: October 10, 2026
Link: https://thestreet.com/employment/business-software-company-hubspot-layoffs-660-jobs-ai-restructuring
Synopsis: HubSpot is eliminating roughly 7 percent of its staff as part of a restructuring to flatten management layers, even though financial results are growing. The CEO told employees on October 6 and said the cuts are not driven by AI efficiencies, while the company also frames its direction around AI-driven outcomes for customers.
Why it matters: A company saying "not about AI" while pivoting to AI is exactly the gap employees fill with their own story.
Suggested action: Comment on LinkedIn with a communications read on how the AI message and the layoff message were sequenced. Check the CEO's October 6 note first; Rex has not seen it.

**2. Few CHROs say they feel very confident they can manage AI-driven change**
Outlet: HR Dive (Ryan Golden) | Published: October 7, 2026
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/few-chros-feel-very-confident-they-can-manage-ai-driven-change/832359/
Synopsis: A Conference Board survey finds that very few CHROs feel highly prepared to lead AI-driven change over the next two years, and only a minority have built AI's expected effects into enterprise-wide planning. Respondents point to difficulty forecasting AI's workforce impact and limited AI skills as the main obstacles.
Why it matters: If leaders cannot yet say what AI means for the workforce, the communication gap to employees starts upstream of the message.
Suggested action: Cite. Ryan Golden is a Wave 2 contact in the tracker; a short comment on his piece is a low-risk first touch.

**3. Executives may use AI more than their workers do**
Outlet: HR Dive (David McCann, first published on CFO.com) | Published: October 6, 2026
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/executives-may-use-ai-more-than-their-workers-do/832141/
Synopsis: Survey research suggests executives use generative and agentic AI more than other employees do. A separate study of senior leaders found a notable share admit to pretending they understand AI better than they do.
Why it matters: Leaders who are unsure themselves are the ones asked to explain AI to everyone else, which makes "last to know" a leadership problem too.
Suggested action: Share with a one-line take. Note the article discloses that Channel V Media, a PR firm, commissioned part of the data; mention that if you cite the figures.

**4. AI adoption may be hampering future organizational growth**
Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen) | Published: October 7, 2026
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-adoption-may-be-hampering-future-organizational-growth/832352/
Synopsis: A Coqual Global Lab report argues companies are adopting AI faster than leaders understand its long-term effects, and that removing entry-level work can erode the hands-on experience that builds future leaders. It recommends understanding existing workflows before automating them.
Why it matters: It supports the argument that telling people what changes, and what development path remains, is a retention and succession issue and not only a morale one.
Suggested action: Cite for the entry-level and development angle.

**5. ACLU files California complaint over Emotify hiring assessment tool**
Outlet: HR Dive (Kathryn Moody) | Published: October 9, 2026
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/aclu-files-california-complaint-over-emotify-hiring-assessment-tool/832596/
Synopsis: The ACLU filed a complaint with California's Civil Rights Department alleging a vendor's emotional-intelligence game assessment may screen out autistic candidates and does not give candidates enough information to ask for an accommodation. The article notes mixed court outcomes for AI hiring tools, including a recent win for Sirius XM.
Why it matters: Regulators and advocates are pressing on transparency to candidates, which mirrors the transparency employees want about AI at work.
Suggested action: Comment, tied to Kathryn Moody (Wave 1 contact).

**6. Sirius XM beats allegations of AI-fueled racial bias**
Outlet: HR Dive (Caroline Colvin) | Published: October 5, 2026 (updated October 6)
Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/sirius-xm-beats-allegations-of-ai-fueled-racial-bias/832115/
Synopsis: A federal court granted summary judgment to Sirius XM in a suit claiming its hiring software disproportionately rejected Black applicants, finding the plaintiff had not shown how the AI use discriminated against him. The vendor said customers decide whether and how to use its AI features.
Why it matters: It is a reminder that legal outcomes will not settle employee trust; communication still has to.
Suggested action: Skip unless you want a regulation thread; item 5 covers the same ground more usefully.

## Pattern of the week
Three of the six items (CHRO confidence, executives overstating AI knowledge, Coqual) describe leaders who are adopting AI faster than they can explain it, which strengthens your lead angle: the communication gap sits with leaders before it reaches employees. The HubSpot story adds the counter-pattern worth watching: a CEO explicitly decoupling a layoff from AI, which may land as reassurance or as the sort of denial that fuels suspicion. Nothing this week cut hard against the angle, but the Sirius XM ruling shows legal exposure for AI use is uneven, so some employers may feel less pressure to explain.
