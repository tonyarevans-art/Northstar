## This week's items

### 1. AI could displace one in 11 workers, Goldman says, but the layoffs are likely waiting for a recession
Outlet: Fortune (Nick Lichtenberg) | Date: October 22, 2026 | Link: https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/goldman-sachs-forecast-how-many-jobs-lost-to-ai-9-percent/

Synopsis: Reporting on a Goldman Sachs economist's note, the piece says the firm's displacement estimate has risen to roughly 9 percent of workers over a decade, but that large-scale job losses historically speed up in downturns rather than in healthy economies. It also notes that AI-attributed layoffs in Challenger's tracking have fallen from their spring peak, and that only 23 percent of U.S. establishments have built AI into regular workflows.

Why it matters: A "wait for the recession" timeline means employees are hearing displacement forecasts long before any announcement, which is exactly the "last to know" gap.

Suggested action: Cite (the figure is below under statistics). Pair it with a post on what leaders should say now, while nothing has been announced.

### 2. The AI productivity boom has bosses asking what's in it for employees
Outlet: Fortune (Emma Burleigh) | Date: October 19, 2026 | Link: https://fortune.com/2026/10/19/ai-productivity-boom-has-bosses-asking-whats-in-it-for-employees/

Synopsis: Employers are starting to ask where the hours AI saves should go. The article describes Indeed pointing saved time toward creative work, Dropbox studying what it calls cognitive atrophy, and Bristol Myers Squibb considering a well-being measure to decide how much of the productivity gain returns to staff, with its chief people officer saying the company has not solved it yet.

Why it matters: Employees want to know what the gains mean for them, and this shows large employers admitting they have no settled answer, which is a communications opening.

Suggested action: Comment. Emma Burleigh is a tracked Wave 2 media contact (weekly digest only until you promote her), so this is a good candidate if you decide to promote her.

### 3. Executives are more likely to trust AI over themselves
Outlet: Axios (Emily Peck), reporting on a Charter survey | Date: October 21, 2026 | Link: https://axios.com/2026/10/21/ai-trust-executives-survey

Synopsis: Charter surveyed 5,002 U.S. office workers who use AI, and executives were about twice as likely as individual contributors to say they trust AI over their own judgment. The article notes this measures self-reported trust, not what executives actually do, and that interviews with workers still showed a persistent tension between AI's benefits and job-loss fears.

Why it matters: A leadership layer that trusts AI more than the people below it widens the exact gap between what leaders know and what employees are told.

Suggested action: Share with a short take on the executive-to-employee trust gap. Cite with the caveat that it is self-reported.

## Pattern of the week

Three of the five pieces (Fortune on productivity gains, Axios on the Charter survey, and the local mental-health report) circle one question: who benefits from AI, and who is told what. Employers are starting to name the "what's in it for employees" question out loud, but the Fortune piece quotes one chief people officer saying the answer is not settled. The Goldman forecast cuts against alarm: it argues the biggest layoffs may be tied to a recession rather than to AI adoption alone, which means the anxiety workers feel may be running ahead of any actual announcement. That supports your angle, since the silence, not the technology, is the exposure. One piece cuts mildly against a simple version of it: the Charter finding that executives lean harder on AI than the people they lead suggests the gap is also about confidence in the tools, not only about information.

A related item just outside the window: BCG published a CEO and employee survey on October 14, 2026 (https://www.bcg.com/press/14october2026-employees-ceo-transparent-ai) in which about 40 percent of employees said their CEO has not been transparent enough about how AI will affect their roles. It is not counted among the items above because it falls outside the seven days.
