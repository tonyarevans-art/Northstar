## Items

### 1. California Enacts New Laws Governing Employer Use of AI
- Outlet: Morgan Lewis (law firm publication, not trade press)
- Date: October 9, 2026
- Link: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/10/california-enacts-new-laws-governing-employer-use-of-ai
- Synopsis: California has added three employer-facing AI laws. One limits relying only on automated systems for discipline or termination and requires a human check and written notice to the employee; another adds technology-displacement details to layoff notices; a third restricts AI tools that infer employees' emotional states. The dates on these differ, with the layoff-notice and surveillance rules starting January 1, 2027 and the human-review rule on July 1, 2027, per the firm.
- Why it matters: The law makes "tell employees what the system is doing and why" a legal duty, which is your lead angle moving from good practice to compliance.
- Suggested action: Cite. A LinkedIn post on what the notice requirements mean for internal communications teams, not just legal teams, would be timely.

### 2. Artificial Intelligence in Employment Newsletter, October 2026
- Outlet: Gibson Dunn (law firm client alert, not trade press)
- Date: October 5, 2026
- Link: https://www.gibsondunn.com/artificial-intelligence-in-employment-newsletter-october-2026/
- Synopsis: The firm maps how state and city rules on automated employment decisions are multiplying and differing from one another, with notice to workers the one obligation they share. It also flags that federal direction is unsettled and that Colorado's rules remain on hold pending a court ruling.
- Why it matters: A patchwork means employers will need one clear, plain-language message to employees that holds across jurisdictions, which is a communications problem before it is a legal one.
- Suggested action: Cite, or skip if you prefer to stay with trade-press sources only.
