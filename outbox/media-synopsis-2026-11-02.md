# Rex's Weekly Media Synopsis, November 2, 2026

Coverage window: October 26 to November 2, 2026. Lead angle: "People do not resist AI. They resist being the last to know what it means for them."

## This week's items

### 1. Companies are not saving money with AI, Gartner finds
- Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen, contributor) | Date: October 29, 2026
- Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/companies-are-not-saving-money-with-ai/873110/
- Synopsis: Gartner research reported by HR Dive says most organizations are not seeing AI cost savings yet, even as boards expect them and most companies plan to spend more next year. Leaders also disagree with each other about how AI helps their workplace.
- Why it matters: When leaders cannot agree internally on what AI is for, employees are the last to hear a clear answer, which is your angle in the data.
- Suggested action: Cite. Pair the leadership-misalignment finding with a post on what employees are told versus what executives expect.

### 2. AI at work: Employees, employers may not be on the same page
- Outlet: HR Dive (Caroline Colvin, reporter) | Date: October 28, 2026
- Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-at-work-employees-employers-may-not-be-on-the-same-page/873055/
- Synopsis: Drawing on Dayforce's annual Pulse of Talent report, the piece shows workers hoping AI-saved time goes to better workflows and learning, while many expect it to mean more work or slower hiring. A large share also worry about losing skills they no longer practice, which Dayforce calls "experience debt."
- Why it matters: It documents the gap between what workers hope and what they expect, a gap that closes only when employers say plainly what happens to saved time.
- Suggested action: Comment or share. Caroline Colvin is a Wave 2 tracked contact (digest only), so note her byline for later promotion.

### 3. What HR needs to know about the AI grief in the workplace
- Outlet: HR Brew (Mikaela Cohen) | Date: October 30, 2026
- Link: https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/what-hr-needs-to-know-about-the-ai-grief-in-the-workplace
- Synopsis: Experts quoted describe some workers grieving the loss of professional identity and career path to AI, and say the fear can show up as errors, burnout, and performative AI use. Their advice to leaders is to communicate honestly, including admitting uncertainty.
- Why it matters: This is the emotional side of your angle, and it backs your point that candor beats polish when employees are afraid.
- Suggested action: Comment. Strong storytelling hook for LinkedIn; not tied to a Wave 1 contact (DeRose is the tracked HR Brew reporter, this is Cohen's piece).

### 4. HR risks losing top performers with voluntary buyout programs
- Outlet: HR Brew (Mikaela Cohen) | Date: October 29, 2026
- Link: https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/hr-risks-losing-top-performers-with-voluntary-buyout-programs
- Synopsis: The article looks at buyouts as a gentler alternative to layoffs, with an expert warning they can push out the people a company most wants to keep. It cites Microsoft's reported buyout offer and says cuts are often funding AI and data center spending rather than responding to distress.
- Why it matters: How a reduction is announced and explained shapes trust, which is the communication-around-big-announcements thread in your topics.
- Suggested action: Share with a short take on announcement communication; skip the company-specific figures unless you verify them at the source.

### 5. Too much disruption can affect workplace culture
- Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen, contributor) | Date: October 28, 2026
- Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/too-much-disruption-can-affect-workplace-culture/872950/
- Synopsis: Citing McLean & Co. and DDI research, the piece says organizations juggling many simultaneous changes feel less capable of building culture, and recommends helping employees tell temporary changes from permanent ones. It also notes few top executives feel confident anticipating change.
- Why it matters: Telling people which changes are temporary is a communication task, which positions your change-communication expertise.
- Suggested action: Cite in a post on change communication.

## Pattern of the week
Three of five items turned on the same gap: leaders expect AI to pay off and employees do not know what it means for them. Gartner's finding that leaders disagree among themselves, Dayforce's finding that workers fear saved time becomes more work, and HR Brew's grief piece all point to unclear communication, not resistance. One item cuts against the angle: the buyout article frames reductions as a cost and retention problem and says little about what employees are told, so the press is still more focused on mechanics than on messaging.
