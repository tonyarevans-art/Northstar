# Rex's Weekly Media Synopsis, November 9, 2026

Coverage window: November 2 to November 9, 2026. Lead angle: "People do not resist AI. They resist being the last to know what it means for them."

## This week's items

### 1. 'AI rebounding': Nearly 20% of U.S. workers replaced by AI are being asked back
- Outlet: Fortune (Joshua Hong, news fellow) | Date: November 9, 2026
- Link: https://fortune.com/2026/11/09/ai-rebounding-20-us-workers-replaced-higher-pay-job-security/
- Synopsis: A survey by assessment firm SHL found that roughly one in five U.S. workers who lost a job to AI were later asked to return. Those returning want higher pay, written job security, and clarity about how AI will be used in their roles.
- Why it matters: Returning workers are asking for transparency about AI before anything else, which is your angle stated by the people living it.
- Suggested action: Cite. Strong LinkedIn storytelling hook on what a returning employee should be told on day one.

### 2. 'Shock absorbers': Korn Ferry says managers are carrying AI change that executives aren't ready to lead
- Outlet: Fortune (Bilin Lin) | Date: November 7, 2026
- Link: https://fortune.com/2026/11/07/korn-ferry-survey-ai-transformation-leaders-middle-managers/
- Synopsis: Korn Ferry's Talent Trends 2027 report finds few leaders believe top executives are prepared to lead AI-driven change, so middle managers are left explaining mandates they did not shape. The piece pairs this with Gallup data linking a clear AI plan and manager support to higher engagement.
- Why it matters: It names the middle-manager communication gap, which is where your internal communications expertise applies most directly.
- Suggested action: Cite and comment. Pair with item 3 for a post on managers as the message channel.

### 3. Workers are facing a 'crisis of disengagement'
- Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen, contributor) | Date: November 6, 2026
- Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/workers-are-facing-a-crisis-of-disengagement/874016/
- Synopsis: Drawing on Glassdoor economist Daniel Zhao's research, the article ties falling engagement to a tight job market, layoff anxiety, burnout, and declining confidence in leadership communication. It also reports that job security is workers' top AI concern, reaching beyond those whose roles AI touches directly.
- Why it matters: Leadership communication is named as a driver of disengagement, which supports positioning clear AI messaging as an engagement lever.
- Suggested action: Share with a short take. Not tied to a tracked contact.

### 4. Nearly two-thirds of HR leaders expect layoffs in 2027
- Outlet: HR Dive (Lara Ewen, contributor) | Date: November 5, 2026
- Link: https://www.hrdive.com/news/hr-leaders-expect-layoffs-in-2027/873880/
- Synopsis: LHH's severance benchmark study, as reported by HR Dive, finds most U.S. HR leaders expect reductions in the next 12 months while severance packages are getting less generous. The report also shows decisions about outplacement shifting away from HR toward finance and procurement.
- Why it matters: As separation decisions move away from HR, communication about layoffs risks becoming a cost conversation rather than a people conversation, and that is a gap communicators can fill.
- Suggested action: Cite. Use the shift in who leads these decisions as a prompt for a post on keeping communication in the room.

### 5. California enacts employment law changes addressing AI, workplace training, and more
- Outlet: DLA Piper (law firm client alert, not trade press) | Date: November 4, 2026
- Link: https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/california-enacts-employment-law-changes-addressing-ai-workplace-training-and-more
- Synopsis: The alert summarizes two California laws. One requires human review and a standalone notice when employers rely mainly on automated tools for employment decisions (effective July 1, 2027), and the other adds a "technology displacement" disclosure to layoff notices when AI or automation substantially causes the cuts (effective January 1, 2027).
- Why it matters: Regulation is starting to require employers to tell workers when AI drives a decision or a layoff, which turns your angle into a compliance expectation.
- Suggested action: Cite after confirming the statute text; this is a law firm summary, not the bills themselves. Consider a post on what a compliant, human layoff message sounds like.

### 6. Most workers say getting good work from AI takes as much effort as training a new hire
- Outlet: WSB-TV Atlanta, republishing Founder Reports (Marc Shorb) | Date: November 3, 2026
- Link: https://www.wsbtv.com/news/most-workers-say/E7RJ4S5HA42N7FP6LOSX7XUSOA/
- Synopsis: A Founder Reports survey of about 2,000 weekly AI users found that six in ten say supervising and correcting AI takes at least as much effort as training a person. Managers report spending more hours on this oversight than individual contributors.
- Why it matters: Hidden AI workload is one of the things employees are not told about up front, which supports honest messaging about what AI changes day to day.
- Suggested action: Skip or cite lightly. It is a vendor-run, self-reported survey republished by a local station, so treat the numbers as directional.

## Pattern of the week
Four of six items point to the same thing from different directions: layoffs, AI mandates, and returning workers all hinge on what employees are told and by whom. Korn Ferry puts the burden on middle managers, Glassdoor on leadership communication, SHL on transparency demands from returning workers, and California on mandated disclosure. One item cuts against the angle: the LHH layoff data shows decision-making migrating toward finance and procurement, which suggests communication may be getting less priority, not more.
